How the maâlems drew it
  1. Rule and compass only. Moroccan tasṭīr (“the art of lines”) starts from a circle divided into equal parts, exactly the constructions of Euclid’s Elements. Turn on Compass circles.
  2. A hidden grid. The circles pack into a tiling of regular polygons that never appears in the finished work. Turn on Hidden grid.
  3. Polygons in contact. From the midpoint of every edge two lines leave at a fixed contact angle; where they meet, stars are born. One angle decides the whole pattern.
  4. Interlace. The lines are widened into straps that pass alternately over and under, never breaking the rhythm.
  5. Zellige. Each enclosed shape becomes a hand-chiselled glazed tile (furmah), set face-down from memory and bound with mortar.
